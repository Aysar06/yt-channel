# Topic discovery and selection
Research date: 6 September 2026. Intended audience: English-language entertainment/gaming/mystery viewers. This is editorial planning, not a forecast of impressions, CTR, retention or revenue.

## Decision
Select **P.T.: the horror demo that became harder to play than escape** (8.75/10). Build the video around the contrast between a tiny, widely remembered game and the loss of ordinary access to it. The story should advance from the anonymous launch, through the Silent Hills reveal, into cancellation, delisting and the preservation problem. The payoff is a precise distinction: Silent Hills never shipped; P.T. did, and surviving installed copies matter. Do not say that Konami remotely deleted every copy.

This narrowly beats the alternatives because it offers a complete narrative, a recognizable single hallway, two strong reversals and a lasting emotional question. P.T. receives only 6/10 for current interest. No evidence establishes a current P.T. search spike. It wins as an evergreen browse idea with a current franchise adjacency.

## Method and limitations
- Generated and scored 17 candidates across every requested dimension. Each score is an editorial 1–10 judgment. Overall score is the unweighted arithmetic mean of 12 dimensions.
- Cu = curiosity; Cl = clickability; Em = emotional reaction; Th = thumbnail potential; Ti = title potential; Re = retention potential; St = story depth; Vi = visual potential; Now = current interest; Ev = evergreen potential; Opp = competition opportunity (higher means more room to stand out); Fit = audience fit.
- Search results and publisher pages were checked on the research date. Dates below are publication dates where returned; video figures are **search-index snapshots**, not guaranteed live counts.
- No callable vidIQ analytics integration was present in the available tool inventory. The recommended plugin was not installed. Public vidIQ statistics pages surfaced but were not treated as connected keyword/VPH analytics.
- Direct Google Trends exploration failed to load. No Trends index, search volume, competition score or rising-query percentage is invented. Web evidence is a proxy for current relevance, not search demand.
- Direct YouTube page opens for the GVMERS and KurtJMac videos failed/throttled; search returned titles and some metadata. This file does **not** claim the first 30 seconds, thumbnails or comments were watched. Those require separate competitor viewing.
- Broad horror interest has a credible platform signal: YouTube reports over 13 billion views of Five Nights at Freddy's-related videos globally from 1 January to 25 September 2025. That is genre context, not a P.T. audience or view prediction. [YouTube, The creators behind the screams](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/horror-gaming/).
- YouTube's [2025 year-end trends summary](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/end-of-year-summary-2025/) describes creator and fan participation around entertainment franchises. This supports considering story expansion and fandom, not a claim that our exact topics currently trend.

## Strongest three

### 1. P.T. — chosen
**Working title:** The Horror Game You Were Never Supposed to Lose  
**Alternative:** P.T.: The Horror Game They Took Away  
**Central question:** How did a free playable teaser turn into a game people are afraid to delete?  
**Promise:** Explain what players experienced, what disappeared, and why the loss still matters.  
**Payoff:** The cancellation and access problem become the final horror twist; distinguish the playable teaser from the imagined full game.

**Evidence:** [Contemporaneous cancellation reporting, Ars Technica, April 2015](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2015/04/signs-points-to-cancellation-for-kojimas-silent-hills/) reports Konami's confirmation and the 29 April removal notice. [Konami, 13 May 2026](https://www.konami.com/games/eu/en/topics/19132/) confirms Silent Hill f exceeded two million cumulative shipments, indicating a living adjacent franchise. This is an adjacency, not proof of P.T. momentum.

**Competitor:** [GVMERS — Kojima's Cancelled Masterpiece - Investigating Silent Hills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_P7wHolvfH0), published 1 September 2018; indexed snapshot 931,676 views. Title sells an unrealized masterpiece and investigation. Our opportunity is to foreground the released artifact and the vanishing ability to experience it, without declaring an unfinished game a proven masterpiece.

**Browse/Homepage hypothesis:** A single doorway plus a vanished download icon creates a clear contrast even for viewers who do not recognize P.T. **Suggested-video hypothesis:** adjacency to Silent Hill retrospectives, Kojima documentaries and lost-game stories. These are editorial hypotheses, not measured traffic-source forecasts.

**Visual feasibility:** Build an explicitly illustrative hallway, short source-backed evidence cards, calendar/availability changes and preservation diagrams. Avoid presenting reconstructed footage as actual P.T. gameplay. Game/creator source excerpts require their own media ledger and editorial review.

**Main constraints:** Do not invent a definitive private reason for the Kojima/Konami split. Do not imply every installed copy was remotely erased. Do not attribute all later first-person horror design to P.T. without developer evidence.

### 2. Backrooms — the real room behind the nightmare
**Working title:** The Internet Found the Backrooms. They Were in Wisconsin.  
**Central question:** Where did a photograph of an ordinary room come from, and how did it become an imagined universe?  
**Promise/payoff:** Trace the source hunt to a mundane physical location, then show how an ordinary image acquired a second life in online fiction and cinema.

**Evidence:** [Wisconsin Frights, source-location reporting](https://www.wisconsinfrights.com/backrooms-photo-location-oshkosh/) documents the HobbyTown connection and original website image; [29 May 2024 community discovery thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/backrooms/comments/1d3olme/backrooms_level_1_original_location_found_not_my/) is a lead to cross-check against the archived site, not sole proof. [A24's official film page](https://a24films.com/films/backrooms) lists the 2026 film. [A24 conversation with Kane Parsons and James Wan, 6 May 2026](https://a24films.com/notes/2026/05/thirty-thousand-square-feet-with-kane-parsons-james-wan) provides first-person production context.

**YouTube interest signal:** [Kane Pixels — Backrooms | Official Teaser HD | A24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BjRndcTYqJo), published 24 February 2026; indexed snapshot 2,625,610 views. A film teaser is an interest signal, not a comparable documentary outlier.

**Opportunity:** Lead with the physical location, not a huge lore encyclopedia. **Why second:** strongest fresh franchise relevance in this shortlist but crowded coverage; source-photo accuracy and differentiating community lore from Kane Parsons' fiction increase research and visual demands. The haunted dimension is fictional; the photo location is real. Any recreation must be labeled.

### 3. Far Lands or Bust — a walk lasting 14.5 years
**Working title:** He Walked Across Minecraft for 14 Years. Then He Got There.  
**Central question:** What makes someone keep walking toward a broken edge of a game for over a decade?  
**Promise/payoff:** An impossible-sounding personal commitment has a real ending, with the community becoming as significant as the destination.

**Evidence:** [KurtJMac's own Far Lands or Bust site](https://farlandsorbust.com/) states he arrived on 4 October 2025 after fourteen and a half years. [KurtJMac's arrival highlights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3gaojqPGbI) are a primary video lead. [PCGamesN, 6 October 2025](https://www.pcgamesn.com/minecraft/far-lands-or-bust-ends-kurtjmac) independently reports the completion. Avoid upgrading the journey into claims that he was the first person ever to reach the Far Lands.

**Opportunity:** Build chapters around the reasons to stop, and why he continued; make the arrival emotional rather than a distance lecture. **Why third:** exceptionally satisfying human story, but the defining payoff is another creator's journey, so commentary must be original and the use of his footage carefully limited or licensed. No current VPH or search spike was obtained.

## Competitor observations grounded in available metadata
- **GVMERS** uses lost-potential framing around a named game and creator. The P.T./Silent Hills title promises investigation and emotional loss. Opportunity: move the preservation problem to the center and keep lore speculation separate.
- **Ahoy** offers unusually deep evidence classification for Polybius. The indexed chapter list reaches its conclusion at 1:06:46; large cumulative views show durable appetite, not current outlier performance. A short summary would struggle to add enough original reporting.
- **All Things Lost** gives Shrek viewers a resolved discovery in the title. Its 412k indexed views are evidence that a narrow lost-animation subject can travel beyond specialists. No channel-baseline ratio was available.
- **nerdSlayer** presents Concord as an entry in an established game-postmortem format. A new video would need a different central question instead of duplicating the shutdown chronology.
- **Kane Pixels and KurtJMac** are primary creators, not interchangeable commentary competitors. Their material validates audience familiarity and provides research leads; it does not grant permission to reuse long sequences.
- No claims here concern competitor thumbnail composition, opening narration, retention curves, audience complaints, or weak timestamps. Those have not been directly observed in this discovery pass.

## Recommended handoff
Proceed to source verification and competitor viewing for P.T. before the script locks. Organize the story around access, disappearance and memory. Use the current Silent Hill franchise only as brief context. Keep the first 30 seconds inside the central contradiction: this game really shipped, people remember it, but a normal new player cannot simply go to the store and download it.
