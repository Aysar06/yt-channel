# Title selection for the P.T. production

## Selected package
**Primary:** The Horror Game You Could Lose Forever  
**Backup 1:** P.T.: The Horror Game You Could Lose Forever  
**Backup 2:** The Game That Outlived Its Own Cancellation  
**One title for an A/B test:** Why People Are Afraid to Delete This Game

The primary earns the strongest editorial mean (9.40/10). It introduces personal stakes without requiring familiarity with the initials P.T. “Could” is essential: this is about losing access to a surviving installation, not a claim that the publisher remotely erased all existing copies.

The thumbnail should identify P.T. clearly so the topic is not concealed for existing horror fans. The opening must fulfill the access promise immediately and distinguish the released teaser from the unreleased Silent Hills.

Backup 1 adds the game name if discovery depends more on franchise recognition or suggested-video adjacency. Backup 2 sells the broader survival paradox, but narration and description must quickly clarify that P.T. survived the cancellation of Silent Hills, the project it teased.

The test title changes the emotional entry from possible loss to the decision to preserve an installation. Use it only if the final narration and source material substantiate players' reluctance to delete P.T.; do not invent direct testimony. Keep the selected thumbnail fixed when testing titles so a result has an interpretable comparison. No test has been run or scheduled.

## Ranking method
The CSV contains 22 titles and scores from 1 to 10 for each required dimension: CTR potential, curiosity, clarity, originality and audience fit. Overall is the unweighted arithmetic mean. These are human editorial judgments, not measured CTR predictions. No connected vidIQ or YouTube testing analytics were available.

The candidates deliberately include curiosity, mystery, story, conflict, revelation, contrarian, transformation, forgotten-story, extreme-event, character-centered, question and simple-statement styles. A stylistic label does not imply the title can override the facts.

## Promise guardrails
- “Lose forever” concerns the ordinary user's copy and official availability; the video must not assert that every file or recording has vanished.
- P.T. was a released playable teaser. Silent Hills was the cancelled game. Preserve that distinction in the script, description and opening graphics.
- “Almost impossible” and “can't simply download again” are about normal official access, not a claim that no preservation or unofficial methods exist.
- “Trapped on old PlayStations” is evocative shorthand and ranks below the primary because it could imply there are no unofficial reconstructions or preservation efforts.
- “Ghost” and “haunts” are story metaphors. No paranormal claim is intended.
- Avoid replacement titles that claim a secret finished masterpiece, a confirmed revenge deletion, a known development budget, or a definitive private reason for cancellation.

## Title-thumbnail pairing
Primary title + **DON'T DELETE** is the strongest complementary pairing: the title asks what can be lost; the thumbnail turns an everyday game-library action into the immediate danger. Include legible P.T. identification on the main game tile or case, separate from the two-word editorial text.

A second thumbnail test can use **NO DOWNLOAD** with an empty game-card silhouette. That alternative emphasizes store absence, while the selected version emphasizes preservation. Test only one variable at a time unless the platform experiment explicitly compares full packages.
