# P.T. thumbnail concepts

Project angle: official access disappears after Silent Hills is cancelled, while existing P.T. installations survive. Target: 1280 × 720, 16:9, high-quality JPG/PNG final. Six concepts were scored. Concepts A and B are now rendered in ../final using an authentic P.T. screenshot, native typography and an editorial deletion symbol. No AI imagery is used. Other concepts remain unrendered proposals.

## A — Don't delete (selected)
**One idea:** deleting this small game can mean losing the experience.  
**Text:** DON'T DELETE (2 words).  
**Main subject:** an authentic P.T. hallway still sourced from Silent Hill Memories, cropped into a large left-side panel. Place a simple red deletion symbol near the card edge, as an editorial graphic rather than a forged PlayStation confirmation screenshot.  
**Composition:** game card fills roughly 55–60% of the frame on the left; two stacked words fill the open right side. Make the P.T. identifier prominent on the card. A warm, illuminated door within the card provides the focal light.  
**Palette:** charcoal/near-black background, dirty warm ivory hallway, white type, one red danger accent.  
**Title pairing:** The Horror Game You Could Lose Forever. The title supplies the stakes; the image presents the vulnerable object and the action.  
**Keep out:** faces, developer portraits, multiple consoles, calendars, paragraph text, fake studio quotes.  
**Accuracy:** the screenshot is genuine; typography and the deletion symbol are editorial additions. Do not imitate an official deletion notice or say “LAST COPY.” Do not imply all copies disappeared.  
**Mobile target:** at 160 × 90, the illuminated card and two-word warning must remain distinct. “P.T.” should still be recognizable even if small supporting details vanish.

## B — No download (first thumbnail alternative)
**One idea:** a famous game no longer has an ordinary download path.  
**Text:** NO DOWNLOAD (2 words).  
**Main subject:** the same authentic hallway image and P.T. identifier as concept A, with the alternative NO DOWNLOAD wording.  
**Composition:** central silhouette against a black void, warm rectangle where the hallway image should have been, text along one side.  
**Palette:** deep black, pale gray, restrained red.  
**Title pairing:** adds an immediate mechanism to “could lose forever.”  
**Risk:** could look like a generic troubleshooting video; the P.T. identifier and horror doorway must retain the narrative context. Do not fabricate a real store screen.  
**Mobile target:** one blank slot and one unmistakable unavailable-download symbol.

## C — The surviving console
**One idea:** the experience survives inside an aging physical machine.  
**Text:** KEEP IT (2 words).  
**Main subject:** one recognizably PS4-shaped, unbranded dark console, with a warm sliver of hallway light suggesting the saved game inside. Use a visual metaphor, not a claim that a door physically emerges from the hardware.  
**Composition:** console low left, bright opening behind/right, empty high area for type.  
**Palette:** blue-black, ivory light, a small red line.  
**Title pairing:** puts “could lose” into a tangible object.  
**Risk:** an unfamiliar viewer might think the video is hardware advice; must identify P.T. with concise typography. Do not show damage unless the script discusses hardware vulnerability.

## D — A door that will not open
**One idea:** an iconic entrance is inaccessible.  
**Text:** none, with P.T. as identification only.  
**Main subject:** one sourced P.T. hallway still ending at a brightly lit door overlaid with a small access-denied symbol.  
**Composition:** strong central vanishing point; doorway in the center third; almost no clutter.  
**Palette:** dirty gold, deep green-black, red access accent.  
**Title pairing:** high atmosphere and mystery with a clear access motif.  
**Risk:** might resemble a generic horror-game upload; lower immediate factual readability. Label reconstruction within the video where used as explanatory footage.

## E — Promise cancelled
**One idea:** the teaser opened onto a future game that never arrived.  
**Text:** CANCELLED (1 word).  
**Main subject:** P.T. hallway card foreground, with a restrained SILENT HILLS wordmark-like text treatment fading behind it.  
**Composition:** diagonal red cancellation stroke touches only the Silent Hills background text, leaving P.T. visible.  
**Palette:** charcoal, cold white, red.  
**Title pairing:** emotional context but repeats a familiar cancelled-project angle.  
**Risk:** easy to misread as P.T. never releasing. Keep the cancellation marker on Silent Hills only, and prefer this concept only with the explicit “They Cancelled Silent Hills. P.T. Survived.” title.

## F — One empty slot
**One idea:** a familiar game-library slot is now an absence.  
**Text:** STILL MISSING (2 words).  
**Main subject:** one bright outlined missing game card on a simplified abstract blue library background.  
**Composition:** card dominates the frame; no actual recognizable account details or fabricated error message.  
**Palette:** dark blue, white, warm gray.  
**Title pairing:** communicates loss cleanly.  
**Risk:** weak emotional subject and could imply the installed file was removed automatically. Caption/narration would need to identify it as the missing store listing; concept remains lower-ranked.

## Final-generation brief for selected concept
Create an original cinematic editorial thumbnail. A single large P.T.-identified horror-game card with an original moody hallway image fills the left portion; the warm-lit doorway is the visual anchor. A minimal red deletion marker makes the risk clear. The right side has large sharp white lettering reading DON'T DELETE, with enough negative space to remain legible on mobile. Dark charcoal background, controlled warm/cold contrast, professional restraint, no extra tiny text, no faces, no watermark, no fabricated official interface. This is a concept illustration, not historical evidence. Preserve 16:9 and export an exact 1280 × 720 final image after visual QA.

## Required verification after generation
Inspect the full image and 320 × 180 / 160 × 90 reductions. Confirm text spelling, the P.T. focal identifier, separation between object and background, no pseudo-text, no extra symbols, and an accurate relationship to the title. No claims of a completed mobile test are made in this concept document.
